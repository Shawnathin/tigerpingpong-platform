# The Tiger PingPong Brand Bible

Version: 1.0
Effective: 2026-07-18
Owner: Shawn Cleve

## The brand in one sentence

Tiger is the Vancouver-born PingPong company that makes serious gear for real life, keeps the game easygoing, and gives people real help without the runaround.

## North star and promise

Internal north star:

> Serious about the gear. Easygoing about the game.

Customer promise:

> Good gear. Real help. No runaround.

These two lines resolve most brand decisions. The product must feel credible. The experience must feel uncomplicated. The company must feel reachable.

## Who Tiger is

Tiger is:

- **Vancouver-born.** Vancouver is the home court, not a decorative backdrop.
- **West Coast in outlook.** Outdoor-minded, relaxed, practical, open, and comfortable with a little weather.
- **Canadian in reach.** Tiger has served and shipped across Canada for more than 15 years. The rest of Canada is not a new frontier.
- **Built around real play.** Homes, patios, basements, garages, schools, universities, community centres, parks, events, and shared spaces matter more than a staged tournament fantasy.
- **Serious about products.** The tone is relaxed because the work behind the gear is not.
- **Human.** Customers can call or email a real person in Vancouver who knows the gear and helps sort things out.
- **Honest about the path.** Tiger began with a table that was not good enough, learned from it, and improved the product rather than airbrushing the beginning.
- **Fun without being frivolous.** PingPong is a social game. Buying it should feel like the beginning of a rally, not an equipment exam.

## Who Tiger is not

Tiger is not:

- A tournament-first performance cult.
- A faceless marketplace or discount warehouse.
- A luxury furniture company pretending the game is secondary.
- A generic “Canadian” costume made of maple leaves, hockey jokes, and forced slang.
- A startup “bringing PingPong to Canada.” Tiger has already been doing the work for more than 15 years.
- A comedian at the customer's expense.
- A brand that hides weak facts behind strong adjectives.
- A company that forces shoppers through a manifesto before helping them choose.

## The founding story

The Tiger story is chapter-based, not a formal dated corporate timeline.

### 1. Start with today

Lead with current Tiger gear in Vancouver. The first impression is capable, modern, outdoor-minded, and established.

Canonical idea:

> Raised on the West Coast.

### 2. Admit the questionable first serve

Tiger started with a skinny-legged table the team was excited about. It was not good enough. Tiger took it to Chinatown nights, rainy events, driveways, and packed rooms anyway. The table revealed what not to do; the people revealed what mattered.

Canonical idea:

> Good energy. Questionable table.

Supporting caption:

> Big dreams. Tiny legs.

Do not name the first table's country of origin. The point is Tiger owning its early choice, not blaming a place or supplier.

### 3. Let Vancouver become the product test

Food Cart Fest, Science World, the Shipyards, schools, universities, community events, corporate events, Whistler, rain, crowds, kids, parties, and spilled drinks turned the city into a practical brief.

Canonical idea:

> The city was our product test.

The proof is real photography and specific places, not a long client list or unsupported dominance claim.

### 4. Explain why Tiger is outdoor-minded

Tiger makes so much outdoor gear because the people behind it are outdoor people. On the West Coast, waiting for perfect weather is not a useful plan. The same resilience also earns a place indoors when kids, parties, moisture, shared use, or busy rooms are part of real life.

Canonical idea:

> Outside is one of our rooms.

Outdoor should never be framed as a compromise product or as useful only on a patio.

### 5. Show how the gear caught up

Tiger stopped settling. Every current Tiger table is made in Germany. For injection-moulded gear, Tiger designs and owns custom moulds and works with specialist manufacturing partners.

Canonical idea:

> So we stopped settling.

This is where the north star belongs:

> Serious about the gear. Easygoing about the game.

### 6. Make the names part of the map

- **Expo** refers to Expo 86 in Vancouver. Its verified theme, “World in Motion—World in Touch,” gives the name a fitting connection to a game built on movement and contact.
- **Whistler** is part of the West Coast landscape and identity. It does not need an invented legend.
- **Portland** reflects rainy patios, independent spirit, and brewery culture. Brewery references belong here when relevant; community centres remain the better default in general customer-use lists.

Do not retrofit a location story onto a product name without owner confirmation.

### 7. Point the story across Canada

The narrative can move through Whistler, UBC, Stampede Park, the Ontario border, the Royal Caribbean ship, and the wrapped truck and trailer. Tiger grew through tables, events, and long drives while continuing to live and work in Vancouver.

The posture is established and neighbourly—not conquest language, not “we are coming for the East,” and not a new national launch.

Canonical idea:

> Born by the Pacific. Helping across Canada.

## The four brand pillars

### 1. Home court

Vancouver explains how Tiger sees the game. Use recognizable places, real events, local weather, and community settings as evidence. “West Coast” is a lived point of view, not a visual filter.

### 2. Gear for real life

Design for how people actually play: families, forgotten paddles, damp garages, busy schools, community centres, backyard BBQs, parties, and shared rooms. Real-life resilience is a customer benefit, not merely an engineering feature.

### 3. The rally is social

The people around the table are the point. Tiger welcomes beginners, families, kids, serious recreational players, campuses, and communities without talking down to any of them.

### 4. Real help

Tiger answers questions like a knowledgeable neighbour. The customer should feel: “Call them. They will help me figure it out.” Service is part of the brand, not a footer utility.

## Audience and occasions

Tiger is for people choosing gear for:

- A backyard, patio, balcony, or cottage.
- A basement, rec room, garage, or busy family space.
- A school, university, community centre, park, or shared building.
- A company, festival, event, or public gathering.
- A player who notices the bounce but does not need the brand to perform seriousness.
- A customer who wants a real answer before or after purchase.

Do not define the audience by age alone. “Youthful” describes Tiger's energy, not an exclusion of older customers.

## Market position

Official competitor sites commonly lead with one or more of these category codes:

- Tournament history, elite players, certifications, and professional performance.
- Technical segmentation by speed, spin, plies, thickness, or player level.
- Manufacturing pedigree, laboratory testing, repairability, and warranty duration.
- Luxury furniture, design status, and premium connection language.
- Catalog breadth, price guarantees, discounts, and promotional urgency.

Tiger can respect those codes without imitating them. Tiger's distinct combination is:

1. A real Vancouver and Canadian story.
2. Outdoor-minded products made for ordinary life indoors and out.
3. Honest, memorable reasons for making each product.
4. Real local help from people who know the gear.
5. A brighter, calmer, more playful shopping experience.

This is a positioning interpretation, not permission to say competitors are inferior, foreign, boring, or uncaring.

## Canadian without the costume

Tiger feels Canadian by being specific:

- Vancouver, the Pacific, Whistler, Expo 86, Canada-wide distances, cottage country, community centres, and real weather.
- Canadian English in house copy: **colour**, **favourite**, **centre**.
- Calm, useful hospitality: “We’ve got you.” “We’ll take it from here.”
- Occasional coast-to-coast cheekiness when it does not make a customer feel like an outsider.

Avoid:

- Maple-leaf wallpaper and generic red-and-white patriotism.
- “Eh,” Mounties, hockey, beavers, or syrup as a default joke.
- West-versus-East insults in shipping, support, or purchase-critical copy.
- Claims that Tiger owns a province, is Canada's largest, or is Canada's leader without evidence.

“Canada's PingPong company” may be used as owner-approved positioning. It must not be expanded into an unsupported market-share or leadership claim.

Vancouver is the source; Canada is the customer map. Seasonal product campaigns may deliberately widen from West Coast identity to a shared Canadian moment—**Summer in Canada**, for example—without pretending every customer lives beside the Pacific.

## How much story belongs where

| Surface                  | Role                               |    Typical allowance |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | -------------------: |
| Homepage hero            | Establish home and reach           |          25–35 words |
| Homepage product support | Give a human reason                |           5–20 words |
| Homepage community proof | Prove local roots                  |          30–45 words |
| Category hero            | Frame the shopping decision        |          18–28 words |
| Category education       | Help someone choose                |          35–55 words |
| Product “Why we made it” | Explain the reason to exist        |          55–75 words |
| Product secondary story  | Reveal a useful use case           |          35–55 words |
| Cart and checkout        | Reassure, do not narrate           | As short as possible |
| Footer                   | Leave one identity signal          |          12–18 words |
| About                    | Tell the complete story with proof |    About two minutes |

One strong personality moment per viewport is enough. A joke in every block turns a personality into a bit.

## Approved recurring lines

Use these deliberately, not as a bag of stickers:

- **Raised on the West Coast.** — primary brand line and About/home territory.
- **Serious about the gear. Easygoing about the game.** — internal north star and selective public proof line.
- **Good gear. Real help. No runaround.** — service promise.
- **The city was our product test.** — Vancouver proof.
- **Outside is one of our rooms.** — outdoor-minded philosophy.
- **Good energy. Questionable table.** — origin chapter.
- **Make a Splash.** — Aqua seasonal territory.
- **Take it Outside.** — Portland/outdoor territory.
- **Ultra Protection.** — Table Cover territory.
- **Need a hand? We’ve got you.** — Contact and service territory.

Do not repeat the same line more than once on one page unless it is part of navigation or a necessary accessible label.

## Lines and behaviours Tiger rejects

Never use:

- “This isn’t your dad’s PingPong company.”
- “We’re not like the other brands.”
- “Game-changing,” “revolutionary,” “best-in-class,” “unmatched,” or “premium” without specific verified evidence.
- “Dominate,” “crush,” “destroy,” or macho challenger language as the default voice.
- A pushy **Our Story** hero CTA when the customer needs a product or a person instead.
- False urgency, fake scarcity, countdown pressure, or sale shouting.
- Profanity in normal public copy. Shawn's candid language informs the attitude; it is not the default published vocabulary.

## The Tiger decision filter

Before publishing, ask:

1. Is it true and sourced?
2. Does it help the customer choose, use, or trust the gear?
3. Does it sound like a knowledgeable Vancouver neighbour rather than a catalog, corporation, or comedian?
4. Is the gear credible without making the game feel precious?
5. Is the personality doing useful work, or merely asking for attention?
6. Could this be shorter?

If the answer to the first question is no or unclear, stop. Voice never outranks truth.
