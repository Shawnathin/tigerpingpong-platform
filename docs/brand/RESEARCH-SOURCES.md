# Tiger Brand Research and Source Register

Version: 1.0
Research date: 2026-07-18

This register records the evidence used to build the brand operating system. It separates Tiger truth from market context and interpretation.

## Research method

The system was derived from:

1. Shawn's owner discovery and page-review decisions across the About, Contact, Homepage, Tables, table-category, and gear-category work.
2. The merged storefront implementation and typed canonical story content.
3. The current custom storefront and legacy Tiger storefront.
4. Owner-cleared historical and lifestyle media already mapped in the repository.
5. First-party competitor sites, used only to understand category conventions.
6. Authoritative Vancouver and Expo 86 records for the Expo name story.

Competitor findings below are interpretation. They do not prove a Tiger product claim or a competitor weakness.

## Market context

### Cornilleau

First-party sources:

- [Cornilleau US](https://us.cornilleau.com/)
- [Cornilleau outdoor performance range](https://us.cornilleau.com/content/54-performance-outdoor-tables-range)
- [Cornilleau institutional tables](https://de.cornilleau.com/en/17-institutional-tables)

Observed emphasis:

- Outdoor durability and weather resistance.
- French manufacturing.
- Laboratory/real-condition testing, safety, warranties, repairability, and spare-parts duration.
- Detailed performance and construction proof.

Tiger implication:

Tiger should verify technical facts just as carefully, but lead customer-facing stories with real-life use, local proof, and the reason the product exists. Tiger must not echo Cornilleau warranty, durability, certification, or repairability claims without Tiger-specific evidence.

### Butterfly

First-party sources:

- [Butterfly North America About](https://butterflyonline.com/about/)
- [Butterfly equipment storefront](https://www.butterflyonline.com/)
- [Butterfly shop](https://shop.butterflyonline.com/shop/?partner=1001BSPSTT)

Observed emphasis:

- High-performance equipment and expert players.
- Professional blades, rubbers, tables, clubs, teams, tournaments, coaches, and research.
- Equipment depth for many styles of competitive play.

Tiger implication:

Tiger can serve players who care about bounce and feel without framing every purchase as advancement toward elite play. Avoid borrowed expert/tournament language unless the specific product and audience require it.

### JOOLA

First-party sources:

- [JOOLA About](https://joola.com/pages/about-us)
- [JOOLA table-tennis tables](https://joola.com/collections/table-tennis-tables)
- [JOOLA table-tennis catalog](https://joola.com/collections/table-tennis)

Observed emphasis:

- German and Olympic/tournament history.
- Professional visibility, athlete relationships, inclusion, innovation, and inspiration.
- Broad catalog with technical filtering.

Tiger implication:

Tiger's proof should come from Vancouver and Canadian real-life play rather than trying to out-history a global tournament brand. A limited catalog should feel curated and generous, not artificially expanded through filter complexity.

### STIGA

First-party source:

- [STIGA US](https://www.stigaus.com/)

Observed emphasis:

- Performance, intermediate, and recreational segmentation.
- Advanced technology, professional endorsements, bundles, promotions, and a large product range.
- Technical paddle and ball language.

Tiger implication:

Tiger should help customers choose by room, use, durability need, and person before defaulting to player-level taxonomy. Technical detail belongs where verified and useful.

### Killerspin

First-party sources:

- [Killerspin About](https://www.killerspin.com/pages/about-us)
- [Killerspin storefront](https://www.killerspin.com/)

Observed emphasis:

- Human connection and unplugging through play.
- Luxury, style, intensity, customization, and design furniture.
- Events, community, education, and mission-led language.

Tiger implication:

Killerspin demonstrates that PingPong can be positioned around people rather than only performance. Tiger's distinct expression must stay grounded in Vancouver, outdoor-minded practicality, accessible product choices, and non-luxury human service rather than borrowing its movement or luxury language.

### Ping Pong Depot

First-party sources:

- [Ping Pong Depot](https://www.pingpongdepot.com/)
- [Ping Pong Depot Canada table listings](https://www.pingpongdepot.com/tables/shipped-to-canada/competition-ittf-approved/)

Observed emphasis:

- Catalog breadth, authorized brands, promotions, sale pricing, and price guarantees.
- Freight and Canadian shipping qualifications.
- Technical and tournament product inventory.

Tiger implication:

Tiger should not compete by reproducing a marketplace wall of products or constant sales language. Tiger's smaller catalog, clearer product reasons, and direct Vancouver help are strengths. Shipping wording must remain as precise as the underlying checkout rule.

## Positioning conclusion

The research does not support saying every other PingPong brand is boring or stuffy. It does show that much of the category communicates through technical performance, tournament authority, manufacturing proof, luxury design, broad inventory, or promotion.

Tiger's credible white space is the combination of:

1. Vancouver and Canadian proof.
2. Outdoor-minded gear for real life indoors and out.
3. Honest “why we made it” product stories.
4. A fun, calm, modern shopping experience.
5. Real local help without the runaround.

That combination is the strategy. “Different” on its own is not.

## Research rules for future contributors

- Use primary sources for technical, historical, legal, and operational facts whenever possible.
- Record the exact URL, access date, and supported claim.
- Do not treat search snippets, retailers, AI summaries, or competitor copy as sufficient Tiger evidence.
- For an owner-origin fact, record Shawn's explicit approval in [FACTS-AND-CLAIMS.md](./FACTS-AND-CLAIMS.md).
- If a source conflicts with the live catalog or another current record, mark the issue for review; do not average the answers.
- Never copy a competitor's phrase, story architecture, campaign idea, or trademarked movement merely because it fits the category.
- Clearly label strategic conclusions as inference.
