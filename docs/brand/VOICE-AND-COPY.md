# Tiger Voice and Copy System

Version: 1.0
Effective: 2026-07-18

## The voice

Tiger sounds like the friend who already knows the gear, understands the room it is going into, and would rather solve the question than turn it into a lecture.

Tiger is:

| More               | Less                     |
| ------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Friendly           | Folksy                   |
| Young in energy    | Childish                 |
| Relaxed            | Careless                 |
| Confident          | Cocky                    |
| Cheeky             | Snarky                   |
| Plainspoken        | Vague                    |
| Helpful            | Pushy                    |
| Product-literate   | Spec-obsessed            |
| Canadian by detail | Canadian by costume      |
| Funny when useful  | Always performing a joke |

The tone is allowed to smile. It is never allowed to make the customer feel stupid.

## House language

### Brand and category spelling

- Always write **Tiger PingPong**.
- Always write **PingPong** as one word in new public copy, including common-noun use: **PingPong table**, **PingPong paddle**, **play PingPong**.
- Preserve third-party titles, quotations, legal names, technical URLs, route slugs, and source identifiers when changing them would be inaccurate or unsafe.
- Use Canadian English: **colour**, **favourite**, **centre**, **neighbour**.

### Point of view

- Tiger is **we**.
- The customer is **you**.
- Prefer direct sentences and contractions: **we’ll**, **it’s**, **you’re**.
- Use **Tiger** when a button or service label is clearer that way: **Call Tiger**, **Email Tiger**.
- Do not refer to Tiger as “the organization,” “the brand,” or “the company” in customer copy unless legally necessary.

### Rhythm

Tiger copy often uses:

- A short eyebrow that frames the moment.
- A confident headline, usually two to seven words.
- One plain-language benefit paragraph.
- One human aside or small turn of phrase.
- A direct action.

Sentence fragments are welcome when they improve pace:

> Rain, dust, leaves, and whatever just blew in sideways.

Parallel fragments work well for place and proof:

> Food Cart Fest. Science World. The Shipyards.

Do not stack fragments until the copy feels like an ad trailer.

### Punctuation

- Periods do most of the work.
- Use an em dash for a conversational turn, not in every sentence.
- One exclamation mark in a moment is usually enough.
- Avoid multiple exclamation marks, all-caps urgency, ellipsis drama, and winking emoji.
- Parentheses should clarify, not carry the joke.

## The five-part Tiger copy pattern

For most shopping sections:

1. **Eyebrow:** tells the customer where they are.
2. **Headline:** makes the idea memorable.
3. **Body:** explains the useful truth.
4. **Proof or quip:** makes the truth feel human.
5. **Action:** states the next step without pressure.

Example:

- Eyebrow: **Outdoor tables**
- Headline: **Take it outside.**
- Body: **Built for backyards and patios. Smart for garages, basements, schools, community centres, and busy game rooms too.**
- Proof: real Portland Outdoor patio photograph
- Action: **Meet Portland**

Not every section needs all five visible elements. Remove an element before adding filler.

## Headline rules

Good Tiger headlines:

- Sound natural when said aloud.
- Make one point.
- Create a small smile or a clear image.
- Leave the body something useful to explain.

Examples:

- **Raised on the West Coast.**
- **Need a hand? We’ve got you.**
- **You’re going to lose a few.**
- **Weather happens.**
- **Meet in the middle.**
- **Something went missing?**
- **Outdoor doesn’t mean outdoors only.**

Avoid headlines that only announce a department, inflate a claim, or perform youthfulness:

- “Welcome to our accessories collection.”
- “Experience unparalleled performance.”
- “Level up your game.”
- “Not your dad’s table tennis.”
- “The ultimate PingPong revolution.”

## Product copy

On category cards and stages, every product needs:

1. A short descriptor.
2. One or two sentences explaining why Tiger made it, who it is for, or what real-life problem it solves.

Category-stage story copy must never exceed two sentences.

Example:

> **Easygoing outdoor.**
>
> We made Expo for backyards that want more playing and less overthinking. It’s the easy yes when you want a real outdoor table and a good time.

That copy is not a replacement for verified specifications. It is the reason to care before the details.

Do not put top thickness, steel gauge, wheel count, performance ratings, warranty, or material claims into the editorial story unless the specific fact is verified and genuinely explains the product's reason to exist.

For the full workflow, use [PRODUCT-STORY-CONTRACT.md](./PRODUCT-STORY-CONTRACT.md).

## Calls to action

Tiger actions are invitations or useful next steps.

Preferred patterns:

- **Find your table**
- **Meet Aqua**
- **Meet Portland**
- **Compare indoor and outdoor**
- **Call Tiger**
- **Email the clues**
- **Cover it up**
- **Get part help**

Use **Explore** for compact category navigation when the destination is obvious. Use a product name when multiple products could be confused.

Avoid:

- **Buy now** as the only story CTA when a gentler, clearer action fits.
- **Learn more** when the destination can be named.
- **Discover our story** in a purchase-first hero.
- Urgent or coercive language without a verified operational reason.

## Tone by surface

### Homepage

Establish Tiger quickly, then let products lead. One Vancouver proof chapter is enough. The homepage should not require the About page to make Tiger feel distinct, but it should not become the About page.

### Category pages

Answer the customer’s first decision, then show the limited catalog generously. Explain category differences in real-life terms. Do not create dense filter theatre when there are only a few good choices.

### Product pages

Lead with the purchase decision and verified essentials. Follow with **Why we made it**, a real-life use story, specifications, compatibility, care, warranty, and FAQ only when facts exist.

### Contact and support

Sound ready, calm, and human:

> Call us. Email us. Tell us what’s going on. We’ll take it from there.

Do not promise hours, response times, stock, replacement-part availability, or outcomes that operations have not confirmed.

### Cart and checkout

Reassure; do not entertain at the expense of clarity.

Approved direction:

> You’re so close to the next rally!

> One more step and we’ll take it from there.

Do not add the company history to an order summary or Stripe handoff.

### Errors, payments, order status, returns, legal, and warranty

Clarity outranks personality. Keep the voice warm, but remove jokes when money, an order, a safety concern, or a right is uncertain.

Good:

> We couldn’t get checkout started. Try again, or give us a call and we’ll help sort it out.

Bad:

> Oopsie! The payment gremlins are at it again.

### Metadata and search copy

Be descriptive first. Use the correct category, use case, Canadian context, and brand. Do not stuff “PingPong,” location names, or superlatives. Metadata must remain factual even when the on-page headline is playful.

## Humour calibration

Tiger humour works best when it:

- Names a familiar truth: balls disappear; weather happens; someone forgets the paddle.
- Comes from the product or situation.
- Makes the customer feel recognized.
- Ends quickly.

Tiger humour fails when it:

- Makes a customer, region, skill level, age, or competitor the punchline.
- Undermines a safety, shipping, compatibility, warranty, or payment message.
- Requires slang Tiger would not naturally say.
- Tries to prove the brand is young.
- Repeats until the useful information is buried.

Public copy should generally be profanity-free. Internal discovery can be candid; published Tiger turns that candour into clean, confident language.

## The Canadian and West Coast dial

Use specificity instead of costume:

Good:

- **Yes, even to cottage country.**
- **Vancouver supplied the weather testing.**
- **Born by the Pacific. Helping across Canada.**
- **If we waited for perfect weather, we’d never get anything done.**

Use carefully:

- East/West coast jokes. They can be playful among neighbours, but not in shipping delays, support problems, or purchase-critical copy.
- Brewery references. They are relevant to Portland's name story and some real use cases; community centres, schools, homes, and shared spaces are the better general default.

Avoid:

- Generic Canadian stereotypes.
- Suggesting eastern customers are an afterthought.
- Calling Tiger new to nationwide shipping.

## Repetition rules

- One strong personality moment per viewport.
- No signature line more than once per page.
- Do not repeat an identical indoor/outdoor explanation on category and product pages. The category teaches the principle; the product explains its specific benefit.
- Do not put “Vancouver,” “Canadian,” or “West Coast” into every product paragraph. Global page context and real photography can carry identity.
- Keep product names out of generic claims when a category explanation is enough.

## Copy review checklist

Before publishing:

- [ ] **PingPong** is one word.
- [ ] Canadian spelling is used.
- [ ] Every claim exists in [FACTS-AND-CLAIMS.md](./FACTS-AND-CLAIMS.md), the live catalog, or a cited product record.
- [ ] Price and availability come from the live catalog.
- [ ] The copy helps someone choose, understand, or get help.
- [ ] The joke, if any, comes from a true situation.
- [ ] The customer is never the punchline.
- [ ] No unverified superlative, warranty, origin, durability, or compatibility claim slipped in.
- [ ] Category-stage product story is no more than two sentences.
- [ ] The CTA names the next step.
- [ ] The copy could not be shortened without losing meaning.
